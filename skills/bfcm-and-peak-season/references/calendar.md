# Peak Moment Calendar

Lead times and mechanics per moment. Dates shift year to year — verify the current year's calendar before building a plan.

**The general rule:** whatever the moment, inventory decisions run 3–6 months ahead, creative runs 8–12 weeks ahead, and audience building runs 6–8 weeks ahead. Everything else is execution.

---

## Q4 / BFCM Block

The dominant moment for most consumer categories.

| Milestone | Timing | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Inventory ordered | May–July | Longer for overseas manufacturing. This is the real deadline |
| Gift guide submissions | May–July | Print and major digital guides work 4–6 months out. See `earned-media` |
| Creative shoot | July–August | Holiday assets, gift sets, seasonal lifestyle |
| Offer decided | September | Depth, exclusions, escalation, early access |
| Creator seeding | September | So content clusters in November |
| List growth push | September–October | The highest-ROI peak activity |
| Gift sets built and live | Early October | Needs to be buyable before gift shopping starts |
| Gifting collections merchandised | Late October | Shop by price, by recipient, gift guides |
| Ads submitted for review | Early November | A Black Friday disapproval is unrecoverable |
| Site load tested | Early November | Peak traffic breaks what worked all year |
| Early access | Mon–Wed of BFCM week | Captures buyers before CPMs peak |
| Black Friday | Late November | |
| Cyber Monday | Following Monday | |
| Extended / last chance | Through the following week | |
| Shipping cutoffs communicated | Early–mid December | Prominent. These convert and their absence generates support volume |
| Gift card push | After the shipping cutoff | Pure margin, saves the late shopper |
| Q5 window | Dec 26 – mid January | Cheap CPMs, gift card redemption, self-gifting, New Year intent |

**Notes:**

- **The season has flattened.** Discounting starts in early November and runs through December. A single-weekend plan under-serves the actual shopping window
- **CPMs rise substantially** from October into December and peak over the BFCM weekend. A discount that is affordable in July may not be affordable against November traffic costs
- **Holding depth flat** across the weekend is simpler, protects margin, and avoids training buyers to wait for Cyber Monday
- **Q5 is the most underused window in ecommerce.** Many brands go dark from December 26 through mid-January, exactly when acquisition costs are at their annual low and New Year intent is at its peak

## Prime Day and Marketplace Events

| Event | Typical timing | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Prime Day | July | Deal submissions run 8–12 weeks ahead. Inventory into FBA even earlier |
| Prime Big Deal Days | October | The second Prime event; a Q4 warm-up |
| Walmart Deals events | Mirrors Prime timing | Increasingly competitive |
| Target Circle Week | Multiple per year | Relevant for brands with Target distribution |

**Marketplace event mechanics differ fundamentally from your own site:**
- Deal submission deadlines are hard and run months ahead
- Inventory must be at the fulfillment center well before the event, and receiving times extend as the event approaches
- Ad costs spike during the event window
- Ranking gains from event velocity persist afterward — this is often the real return, more than the event-day revenue itself

See `amazon-growth` and `marketplace-ads`.

## Category Moments

| Moment | Timing | Categories |
|---|---|---|
| New Year / resets | Jan 1–31 | Fitness, wellness, supplements, organization, planners |
| Valentine's Day | Feb 14 | Jewelry, flowers, confectionery, lingerie, gifting, experiences |
| Spring refresh | March–April | Home, garden, cleaning, apparel |
| Easter | March–April | Confectionery, kids, gifting |
| Mother's Day | May | Jewelry, beauty, flowers, gifting — often the second-largest gifting moment of the year |
| Father's Day | June | Tools, grooming, tech, apparel |
| Summer | May–July | Swim, outdoor, travel, sun care, beverage |
| Back to school | July–August | Kids, apparel, supplies, tech, lunch |
| Halloween | October | Costume, confectionery, decor |
| Singles' Day (11/11) | November | Relevant for brands selling into Asia-Pacific |
| Small Business Saturday | Saturday after Thanksgiving | Strong angle for genuinely small brands |
| Green Monday | Second Monday in December | The last big pre-shipping-cutoff push |

**Gifting moments have a common structure**, and it is worth building the pattern once and reusing it:

1. The buyer is not the user. Copy must speak to the gifter, and address their uncertainty about the recipient's preference
2. Price-point clarity matters more than usual — shop-by-price collections outperform category collections
3. Presentation matters. Gift packaging, gift messaging, and gift receipts all convert
4. Extended returns for gift purchases, communicated clearly
5. Shipping deadlines are the conversion driver in the final week
6. Gift cards catch everyone who missed the cutoff

## Non-Gifting Categories

If the brand sells a replenishment consumable with no gifting angle, forcing a BFCM promotion often destroys margin without adding customers — it discounts the buyers who would have reordered anyway.

Better plays for these categories:

- **Subscription push** rather than a discount. Peak is a strong moment to convert one-time buyers to subscribers, and it captures the value permanently. See `subscriptions-and-replenishment`
- **Stock-up offers** — a genuine multi-pack value, which raises AOV without resetting the unit price anchor. See `bundles-and-aov`
- **Gift set versions** of the core product, which creates a gifting angle that did not exist
- **Skip Q4 promotionally** and spend the budget in Q5 when CPMs are low and New Year intent is high

The decision to sit out a peak moment is legitimate and should be made deliberately, with the margin model to support it — not by default.

## Post-Peak Debrief

Run it in January, while the detail is still recoverable. It is next year's plan.

- Revenue by week versus plan
- **Contribution margin by week** — the number that says whether it actually worked
- Discount depth actually realized versus planned
- What stocked out, when, and what that cost in lost revenue
- What was left over, and what clearing it cost
- CPM and CPA by week versus last year
- New customer count, and their repeat rate over the following 90 days
- **Lead-time failures**: what was late, and what deadline it should have hit

The last item is the most valuable. Peak-season failures are almost always lead-time failures, and they repeat every year until someone writes them down.
